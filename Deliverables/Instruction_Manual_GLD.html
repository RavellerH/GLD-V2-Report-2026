1.Introduction

The GLD (Gas Leak Detector) is an IoT-based, multi-gas leak detection sensor node developed by LAPI Ganesha Utama (LGU) together with the IoT Lab and Physics Lab of Institut Teknologi Bandung (ITB), under the GLD Tahap 2 program for PT Pertamina Patra Niaga. GLD is intended to detect gas leakage at an early stage in refinery facilities and to report gas concentration status wirelessly to a central monitoring dashboard, helping to prevent incidents such as fires and explosions.

Unlike a conventional loop-powered gas detector with a local 4–20 mA analog output and an on-unit keypad/display, GLD is a wireless sensor node: it classifies gas conditions on-device and reports status over a LoRa mesh network (via a Cluster Head and Gateway) to a central server and dashboard. GLD has no physical keypad or digital concentration display; status is conveyed through a local visual/audible alarm module and remotely through the dashboard.

GLD currently classifies ambient air into three conditions on-device: Clean Air, LPG, and H₂ (hydrogen). It does not yet cover CO₂, Benzene, CO, or H₂S; expanding gas coverage is an active area of the program (see Section 13, Specifications).

GLD is not yet certified for use in ATEX/IECEx classified hazardous areas. Certification is in progress — see Section 14, Certification Status. Do not install GLD in a classified hazardous area until certification is complete and the equipment has been confirmed suitable for the applicable zone, gas group, and temperature class.

2.Safety Precautions

Warning
  • Do not install GLD in a classified (ATEX/IECEx) hazardous area until certification of the equipment for the applicable zone, gas group, and temperature class has been completed and confirmed. GLD's certification is currently in progress (Section 14).
  • Any cable entry device and blanking element used with GLD in a hazardous area must be of a type of protection and certification suitable for the intended zone and correctly installed. GLD's own cable entry specification is currently Pending confirmation (Section 13); do not assume a specific cable gland is suitable without written confirmation.
  • Do not disassemble the product or modify its enclosure, wiring, or firmware. Doing so may compromise any explosion-protection measures once certification is finalized, and will void any future certification coverage.
  • Be sure to ground the unit in accordance with the site's electrical safety requirements to prevent electric shock.
  • Only trained and authorized personnel may install, commission, operate, inspect, or maintain GLD.
  • Isolate and verify the 24 VDC supply before opening the enclosure or changing wiring. Apply the site lockout/tagout (LOTO) procedure.
  • Perform alarm tests with appropriate site controls and hearing protection where an audible alarm device is connected.
  • Perform sensor nulling only in confirmed clean air, and accept the result only when all eight sensors report a complete 8/8 pass (see Section 9, Operating Modes); otherwise correct the cause and repeat.
  • Remove the unit from service if there is visible damage, water ingress, an unresolved sensor fault, failed alarm proof, or a loss of protective-earth continuity.
Caution
  • All work involving GLD — including installation, wiring, and mounting — must be carried out by suitably trained personnel in accordance with the applicable code of practice and site HSE procedures.
  • Do not install GLD near sources of silicone sealant vapor or other substances known to poison metal-oxide gas sensors; this may permanently degrade sensor performance.
  • Handle the sensor unit and enclosure with care. The gas-sensing elements are directly exposed to ambient air (not enclosed) and can be damaged by physical impact, dust ingress, or contact with liquids.
  • Mounting to existing plant structures must not involve drilling or welding unless a different method has been separately approved in writing by the site (RU IV). See Section 5, Installation.
  • Fasteners used for mounting must use a material and grade suitable for the site environment (anti-corrosion required); the specific fastener grade for GLD's bracket is currently Pending confirmation (Section 13).
  • Do not create new cable entries, enlarge an entry, leave unused entries open, or substitute a general-purpose gland once a certified entry device is specified (Section 13).
Note

GLD's gas-sensing elements (metal-oxide type) are intentionally exposed to ambient air through the enclosure mesh, in the same manner as comparable diffusion-type gas detectors, in order to sense gas concentration. The ignition-protection status of this sensing aperture (e.g. whether a certified flame-arrestor element is required) is under evaluation as part of the ongoing ATEX/IECEx certification process and is not yet finalized.

5.2 Mounting Location and Height

GLD is designed to detect multiple gases with different vapor densities (for example, LPG is heavier than air, while H₂ is lighter than air). Unlike a single-gas detector, a single mounting height cannot simultaneously be optimal for every gas GLD may be asked to detect. The mounting height and location for each installation point should therefore be selected by site engineering based on the specific target gas(es) and hazard assessment for that location, rather than a single fixed rule.

General siting guidance (adapted from established gas-detector practice, to be applied per site hazard assessment):

Relative gas densityGeneral guidance
Heavier than air (e.g., LPG)Mount low, close to grade or process equipment where the gas is expected to accumulate.
Similar to air (e.g., carbon monoxide)Mount at approximately breathing height, considering local air movement.
Lighter than air (e.g., hydrogen)Mount high, near the ceiling or upper structure where the gas is expected to accumulate.

Do not install GLD in locations where: the ambient temperature exceeds the specified operating range (Section 13); condensation regularly occurs; water is directly sprayed; the atmosphere is corrosive beyond the specified rating; or interfering hydrocarbon gases other than the intended target gas are continuously present. Provide sufficient clearance around the enclosure for cable access and maintenance.

In Case of a Gas Leak Alarm

Warning
  • Follow your site's emergency response procedures immediately upon a gas leak alarm.
  • If the leak occurs indoors, ventilate the area in accordance with site procedure.
  • Do not operate electrical switches in or near the affected area; sparks may cause ignition.
  • Confirm safety with a calibrated portable gas detector before re-entering the affected area, in accordance with site procedure.

9.Gas Detection and Alarm Operation

GLD uses eight metal-oxide gas sensor channels (MQ-2, MQ-3B, MQ-4, MQ-5, MQ-6, MQ-7B, MQ-8, MQ-135), with sensing elements directly exposed to ambient air. Signals are digitized by a 24-bit multi-channel ADC (ADS1256IDBR, 30,000 SPS) and sampled at a 500 ms scan interval. An on-device AI classifier running on the ESP32-S3 processor evaluates the sensor pattern and outputs one of three classes: Clean Air, LPG, or H₂. A dedicated SHT40 sensor provides auxiliary temperature/humidity telemetry.

Current alarm logic: an alarm is raised when the classified condition is not Clean Air and the classification confidence is at least 30%.

On alarm, two parallel channels are activated: (1) the local visual/audible alarm module on the unit itself, and (2) a push notification transmitted over the LoRa network to the central dashboard, targeting a cycle time of approximately 10 seconds. Under normal (non-alarm) conditions, the system uses a longer polling interval (approximately 90 seconds) to conserve network bandwidth.

Validation status

The push-alarm pathway has been successfully validated on a campus mesh test network. Validation of this pathway at an actual refinery (RU) installation has not yet been performed.

Note

The current 3-class model does not yet cover CO₂, Benzene, CO, or H₂S. Expansion of gas coverage depends on the availability of reference gas samples for on-site training (see Section 11, Maintenance).

Treat an alarm as a real process-safety event until the site response procedure establishes otherwise.

10.Status Indication and Fault Conditions

GLD has no on-unit digital display or numeric fault-code readout comparable to a conventional stand-alone gas detector. Status and fault information are available through two channels:

  • Local alarm module — visual (flashing) and audible indication of an active gas alarm.
  • Remote dashboard / RS-485 status register — device status word, classification result, confidence, battery voltage, power source, and external power indicator, available via the central dashboard or by polling the RS-485/Modbus register set described in Section 7.

A dedicated on-unit fault/error code table, of the kind found in conventional stand-alone gas detectors, has not been defined for GLD. Fault diagnosis currently relies on the dashboard and/or the RS-485 status word; see Section 12, Troubleshooting, for the fault conditions that are understood at this time.

11.2 Sensor Response Verification

GLD's sensor response is verified using the project's portable gas test chamber and reference gas samples, following the project's on-site chamber-based methodology: the chamber is brought to the deployment location, a controlled gas sample is introduced, and the sensor/classifier response is checked and, where needed, the on-device model is retrained using the locally captured data. This differs from the zero/span calibration procedure used by conventional analog gas detectors, since GLD does not have an analog output to calibrate; instead, its AI classifier is verified and retrained against reference gas exposure.

Before returning a unit to Inference mode after verification or maintenance, run Nulling mode (Section 9.1) in confirmed clean air. Accept the result only when all eight sensors report a complete 8/8 pass; otherwise correct the cause and repeat before placing the unit back in service.

11.3 Sensor Replacement and Service Life

The metal-oxide (MQ-series) sensing elements used in GLD are consumable components with a finite service life, as is typical for this sensor technology. A specific recommended replacement interval for GLD's sensors has not yet been established and should not be assumed to match the service life figures published for other sensor technologies (e.g., electrochemical or NDIR sensors used in other detector models). See Section 16, Service Life.

The −40°C to +85°C figure above is a project-confirmed value corresponding to a typical extended-industrial electronics component rating. Whether −40°C/+85°C is intended as GLD's true field deployment target, or is a component rating rather than a field specification, should be confirmed before this value is relied upon in a certification submission.

16.Service Life

An overall design service life and a specific gas-sensor replacement interval have not yet been established for GLD. As with any metal-oxide gas sensor, sensing performance is expected to degrade over time and with exposure to high-concentration or contaminating gases; the sensing elements should be treated as consumable components requiring periodic verification (Section 11) and eventual replacement, on an interval to be defined once field experience is available.
